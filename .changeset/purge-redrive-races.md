Suspended-stage polls, `run.redrive` and `run.purge` lose less data to writers that race them.

- `stage.pollSuspended` no longer fails a stage that a `job.execute` completed while the poll was running. A stale `checkCompletion` that returned or threw an error, a deadline that elapsed, or a durable replay that ran out of attempts used to write FAILED over the COMPLETED stage and fail its run.
- More generally, a `checkCompletion` or durable replay that resolves after a `job.execute`, a cancel, a redrive or another poller wrote the stage commits nothing: every stage write is fenced on the version the poll claimed, it reads the stage row once more before its first write, and it is not counted as resumed. A failed read does not fail the stage; the fence decides. A poller that cannot serve the stage's workflow or resume strategy fences its FAILED write the same way. What user code writes itself (`ctx.storage`, `ctx.log`, step rows) is not covered. The in-memory persistence now throws `StaleVersionError`, as the Prisma adapter does, for a version-fenced `updateStage` on a deleted row.
- A durable replay that throws reads its attempt budget from the stage's job row even while that row is still RUNNING (the host has not yet acknowledged the suspension). Previously it fell back to three attempts, so a job configured for one attempt was retried. The replay's retry write is fenced on the stage version too.
- `run.redrive` fences its run update on the version whose status it checked, so a run cancelled or redriven meanwhile is refused; `run.rerunFrom` checks COMPLETED/FAILED on that same read. It releases the signal wake keys of the stage records it deletes, which nothing released before. Its post-commit blob cleanup keeps every key an `outputData` points at, the deleted records' and the current ones' (read after each stage prefix is listed), so a new attempt that completed before the cleanup keeps its output and a caller who rolls back the redrive's transaction keeps the restored records' output.
- `run.purge` cleans the stage records a run has when the sweep re-reads it, not the ones selection saw, and repeats its cleanup once after deleting the run. A post-commit enqueue (`run.claimPending`, `run.transition`, `run.redrive`, `run.reapStuck`) that lands after the run was deleted removes its jobs and their spilled payloads, and a signal whose stage record was deleted while it was delivering removes its ledger row, spilled payload and wake key.

Still open, as before: a redrive, rerun or signal is not held off while a purge runs; a failed post-commit ledger clear of a deleted record leaves that record's external rows; output written to a reused key during a redrive's cleanup can be deleted. The docs list these limits.

No port changes. Statements measured on a one-stage run (create, claim, `job.execute`, transition, redrive from the start, cancel, purge), a1faef9 then this release. `job.execute` is unchanged: 18 on SQLite with Prisma 6 and 7, 28 on Prisma 8 SQLite, 22 on Postgres with Prisma 6, 20 with Prisma 7 and 8. `run.redrive` adds one: 20 to 21 on SQLite 6 and 7, 24 to 25 on SQLite 8, 21 to 22 on Postgres 6, 7 and 8. `run.purge` of one run: 15 to 24 on SQLite 6 and 7, 17 to 26 on SQLite 8, 15 to 24 on Postgres 6, 7 and 8 (the stage read and the repeat after deletion). Each post-commit enqueue reads the run once more.
