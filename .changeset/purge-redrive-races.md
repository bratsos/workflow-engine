`run.purge` no longer deletes data a redriven run still needs, and no longer leaves data behind from writers that race it.

- Before it touches a run, the sweep takes a per-run purge claim (an idempotency key of type `run.purge.claim`) and re-reads the run and its current stage records. Previously a redrive that landed during the sweep kept the run but lost the blobs, ledger rows or job the sweep had already deleted, and a run redriven and finished again between selection and the sweep was cleaned with its old stage record ids, leaving the new record's spilled step results and signal wake keys behind.
- While the claim is held, `run.redrive`, `run.rerunFrom` and a `step.signal` wake on that run reject with "is being deleted by run.purge". Retry them once the purge has finished. Only a purge takes a claim over: a sweep that fails part-way keeps the run refused and the next purge finishes it at once; a claim left by a crashed sweep can be taken over by a purge after 10 minutes, and a sweep still running at that age stops before its next delete and leaves the claim alone. Redrive and a signal's wake hold the claim for their whole transaction, which also serializes them on the in-memory persistence. Kernels from before this release ignore the claim.
- After deleting a run, the sweep repeats its cleanup once. A post-commit enqueue (`run.claimPending`, `run.transition`, `run.redrive`, `run.reapStuck`) that lands after the run was deleted removes its jobs and their spilled payloads, and a signal whose stage record was deleted while it was delivering removes its ledger row, spilled payload and wake key.
- `run.redrive` releases the signal wake keys of the stage records it deletes. They were never released, since neither a later signal nor `run.purge` can find a deleted record.
- `run.rerunFrom` checks COMPLETED/FAILED on the same read its run update is fenced on, so a run cancelled while it was deciding is refused instead of redriven.

- `stage.pollSuspended` no longer fails a stage that a `job.execute` completed while the poll was running. A stale `checkCompletion` that returned or threw an error, a deadline that elapsed, or a durable replay that ran out of attempts used to write FAILED over the COMPLETED stage and fail its run. The failure is now fenced on the stage row's version and the poll moves on.
- More generally, a `checkCompletion` or durable replay that resolves after a `job.execute`, a cancel, a redrive or another poller wrote the stage now writes nothing of its own: no stage update, output, artifacts, annotations or events, and it is not counted as resumed. Each poll reads the stage row once more before its first write; if that read fails the poll goes on and its fenced write decides, so a read error never fails the stage. Output and artifacts a poll writes go under a `poll-<stageRecordId>-<version>/` folder of the stage prefix and are removed if its commit loses, and a durable replay whose later write or commit fails removes what it already wrote there. Code that loads a poll-completed stage's output from the default key (`ctx.storage.getStageKey(stageId)`) must read `outputData._artifactKey` instead, as `ctx.require` and the workflow context already do. What user code writes itself (`ctx.storage`, `ctx.log`, step rows) is not covered. The in-memory persistence now throws `StaleVersionError`, as the Prisma adapter does, for a version-fenced `updateStage` on a deleted row.

- A redrive that deletes stage records now writes their ids, in its transaction, to an idempotency record (`run.redrive.pendingLedgerClear`, keyed by the run id) and releases their signal wake keys there too. Its post-commit step removes only the ids it cleared, after the ledger clear and the deletion of their spilled step results both succeeded. If either fails, the ids stay and `run.purge` clears those ledgers and spilled step results before it reports the run deleted. Previously the checkpoint and spill of such a record outlived the purge.
- A redrive's blob cleanup of deleted records keeps every key an `outputData` points at, the deleted records' and the current ones' (read after each stage prefix is listed), and never deletes the poll folder of a record it did not delete. A new attempt that completed before the cleanup reached its stage keeps its output, and a redrive run on a caller's transaction that the caller rolls back no longer leaves the restored records pointing at deleted output. Keys a stage wrote itself through `ctx.storage` under its stage prefix, and output a new attempt wrote to the stage's shared keys before the listing but committed after it, are still deleted by the cleanup.
- A poller that cannot serve a suspended stage's workflow or resume strategy fences its FAILED write on the claimed version, so a host that completed the stage since the claim keeps it.
- A durable replay that throws now reads its attempt budget from the stage's job row even while that row is still RUNNING (the host has not yet acknowledged the suspension). Previously it fell back to three attempts, so a job configured for one attempt was retried. The replay's retry write is also fenced on the stage version, so a stale replay that throws after a job completed the stage no longer bumps its attempt, error or suspension state, and no longer emits `stage:retrying`.

No port changes. Statements measured on a one-stage run (create, claim, `job.execute`, transition, redrive from the start, cancel, purge), a1faef9 then this release: `job.execute` 18/18 on SQLite with Prisma 6 and 7, 28/28 on Prisma 8 SQLite, 22/22 on Postgres with Prisma 6, 20/20 with Prisma 7 and 8 (unchanged). `run.redrive` 20 to 29 on SQLite 6 and 7, 24 to 35 on SQLite 8, 21 to 32 on Postgres 6, 21 to 30 on Postgres 7 and 8. `run.purge` 15 to 27 on SQLite 6 and 7, 17 to 33 on SQLite 8, 15 to 31 on Postgres 6, 15 to 27 on Postgres 7 and 8. Each post-commit enqueue reads the run once more.
