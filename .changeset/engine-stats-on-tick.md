New read-only command `engine.stats` reports the state of active work without the engine taking a metrics dependency: runs, stages and jobs by non-terminal status, the age of the oldest PENDING run and job, the SUSPENDED stages that are due and how overdue the most overdue one is, unpublished outbox events and the age of the oldest, and dead-lettered events. It counts PENDING, RUNNING and SUSPENDED rows only. The Prisma adapters answer each figure from an index that leads with the active column, so its cost follows the work in flight and not the table size when the tables were never analysed or their statistics are current (asserted on SQLite and on PostgreSQL in both states, at 500,000 published outbox events and 100,000 terminal rows per table). Statistics that are out of date are the exception on PostgreSQL. When `ANALYZE` ran while most rows were active and the tables then grew by terminal rows without another (autovacuum off or behind, or a bulk load after the last `ANALYZE`), the planner still believes most rows are active and reads the whole table for the runs, jobs and unpublished-outbox counts: on Prisma 7 with 100,000 terminal rows per table, these counts used 1,725, 1,640 and 1,430 buffers, respectively (177, 165 and 144 at 10,000). The stage count used 10 buffers in this Prisma 7 measurement. The due-stage and dead-letter counts each used 3. Planner choices depend on the stored estimates. `ANALYZE workflow_runs, workflow_stages, job_queue, outbox_events` restores the index plans. SQLite is bounded in that state too: its statements mark the active-status, unpublished, due and dead-letter terms as rare with `likelihood()`, so the planner searches the index whatever `sqlite_stat1` says (asserted at 10,000 and 100,000 rows on Prisma 6, 7 and 8). Exactly: the dead-lettered count reads `outbox_events_dlqAt_idx` from its first non-NULL entry, and on PostgreSQL counts events whose `dlqAt` lies between 1970-01-01 and 9999-12-31 (the bounds that make the planner choose that index without statistics; the engine never writes one outside them; a dead letter stamped before 1970 by a test clock is not counted); the due-stage count uses `workflow_stages_status_idx`, or `workflow_stages_nextPollAt_idx` on PostgreSQL once its statistics say few stages carry a deadline, which holds a terminal stage only until its job is settled. It writes nothing: no outbox event, no idempotency key, no transaction.

It reads two new optional port methods, `Persistence.getActiveWorkStats(now)` and `JobTransport.getJobStats()` (also on `JobQueue`). Nothing is required of a custom adapter: a port without a method has its sections reported as `{ available: false }` instead of failing or reading as zero. The in-memory ports and the Prisma adapters (Prisma 6, 7 and 8, on PostgreSQL and SQLite) implement them, and `persistenceConformanceSuite` and `jobQueueConformanceSuite` cover them: an adapter without the methods passes those tests, which return early when the method is absent and are not reported as skipped. `createSpillingJobTransport` forwards `getJobStats` when the transport it wraps has it. `runTickHook(hook, counts, logPrefix, timeoutMs?)` and `MaintenanceTickHook` are exported from `@bratsos/workflow-engine/kernel` for hosts that call an `onTick` hook themselves: the hook gets a copy of the counts, a throw or rejection is caught and logged, and with `timeoutMs` the call stops waiting after that long, logs one warning and leaves the hook running.

`KernelCommand` and `CommandResult` gain `engine.stats`: a custom host or wrapper with an exhaustive `switch` over `command.type` stops compiling (`Type 'EngineStatsCommand' is not assignable to type 'never'`) until it handles the new case or has a `default`.
