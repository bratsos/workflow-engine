New read-only command `engine.stats` reports the state of active work without the engine taking a metrics dependency: runs, stages and jobs by non-terminal status, the age of the oldest PENDING run and job, the SUSPENDED stages that are due and how overdue the most overdue one is, unpublished outbox events and the age of the oldest, and dead-lettered events. It counts PENDING, RUNNING and SUSPENDED rows only. The Prisma adapters answer each figure from an index that leads with the active column, so its cost follows the work in flight and not the table size, with or without planner statistics (asserted on SQLite without statistics and on PostgreSQL for tables never analysed and after `ANALYZE`, at 500,000 published outbox events and 100,000 terminal rows per table). Exactly: the dead-lettered count reads `outbox_events_dlqAt_idx` from its first non-NULL entry, and on PostgreSQL counts events whose `dlqAt` lies between 1970-01-01 and 9999-12-31 (the bounds that make the planner choose that index without statistics; the engine never writes one outside them); the due-stage count uses `workflow_stages_status_idx`, or `workflow_stages_nextPollAt_idx` on PostgreSQL once its statistics say few stages carry a deadline, which holds a terminal stage only until its job is settled. It writes nothing: no outbox event, no idempotency key, no transaction.

It reads two new optional port methods, `Persistence.getActiveWorkStats(now)` and `JobTransport.getJobStats()` (also on `JobQueue`). Nothing is required of a custom adapter: a port without a method has its sections reported as `{ available: false }` instead of failing or reading as zero. The in-memory ports and the Prisma adapters (Prisma 6, 7 and 8, on PostgreSQL and SQLite) implement them, and `persistenceConformanceSuite` and `jobQueueConformanceSuite` cover them: an adapter without the methods passes those tests, which return early when the method is absent and are not reported as skipped. `createSpillingJobTransport` forwards `getJobStats` when the transport it wraps has it. `runTickHook(hook, counts, logPrefix, timeoutMs?)` and `MaintenanceTickHook` are exported from `@bratsos/workflow-engine/kernel` for hosts that call an `onTick` hook themselves: the hook gets a copy of the counts, a throw or rejection is caught and logged, and with `timeoutMs` the call stops waiting after that long, logs one warning and leaves the hook running.
