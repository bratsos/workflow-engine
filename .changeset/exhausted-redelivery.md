"@bratsos/workflow-engine": patch
---

A job delivered again after its stage already ended `FAILED` no longer reruns the stage. A stage is `FAILED` when its final attempt threw or its error was deterministic; if the host then lost the acknowledgement (a crash before `jobTransport.fail`, a lost lease, a delivery past the budget such as attempt 3 of 2, or a delivery within the budget after a deterministic failure on attempt 1 of 3), the redelivery reset the stage's step ledger and ran the completed steps again: a receipt with an external key ran a second time under the same key, or, with `onReclaim: "fail"`, its successful row was turned into a failure that replaced the real error. `job.execute` now answers a delivery that carries `attempt` and finds its stage `FAILED` with `outcome: "failed"`, the stored error, `willRetry: false` and no events, before it starts anything: the host fails the job and the run fails once with the stage's error, and no retry is scheduled; the stage, its ledger and its events are left as the first delivery wrote them. A retry in budget after a retryable failure is unchanged (the stage is `PENDING`, completed steps replay), and a direct `job.execute` dispatch without `attempt` still starts a `FAILED` stage again as an explicit re-run.

An attempt that was overtaken while its body ran no longer writes over the stage, and its output no longer replaces the output of the attempt that took over. `job.execute` fences the stage write that ends an attempt (completed, suspended, a pending retry or `FAILED`) on the stage version its start wrote, folded into that write's `WHERE`. That version is the delivery's ownership of the stage: every later write to a `RUNNING` stage comes from a new owner (another delivery after a lost lease, a redrive, `run.rerunFrom`, a cancel, `run.reapStuck`), and `step.signal` now leaves a `RUNNING` stage's row alone (its nudge was overwritten by the attempt's own `nextPollAt` anyway). So after a cancel and a redrive a late failure can neither mark the reopened stage `FAILED` (which the redrive's own job would then have taken for a dead job) nor complete it, and after a lost lease the stale attempt can no longer commit over the delivery that took over (which left the run stuck until `run.reapStuck`). The late result is discarded with `ghost: true` and `ghostReason: "orphan"`, and its host's fenced acknowledgement leaves the new owner's job alone. If the row was written by something that does not own it, such as `step.signal` of an earlier release during a rolling deploy, the result is discarded with `ghostReason: "race"` and the host re-delivers the job, which runs the stage again.

Stage output and artifacts are now saved under keys of the attempt's own, `workflow-v2/<type>/<run>/<stage>/attempts/<id>/output.json` and `…/attempts/<id>/artifacts/<name>.json`, in `job.execute` and in both completion paths of `stage.pollSuspended`. Only the committed stage write points `outputData` at them, so an attempt or poll that loses its fence (also on earlier releases, where it overwrote the winner's blob under the shared per-stage key and the next stage read it) never changes what the next stage, `loadWorkflowContext` or any reader of the pointer gets. The loser's keys are deleted afterwards, best-effort, unless some `outputData` points at them; anything left goes with `run.purge`, and with a redrive that deletes the stage record. A redrive that reopens a stage keeps its earlier attempts' blobs, so the archived pointer of a superseded attempt still names its output. Pointers stored by an earlier release name the old per-stage key and are still read as they are. `saveStageOutput` and `saveStageArtifacts` take an optional attempt key (a random one by default). The engine no longer writes the stage output to `ctx.storage.getStageKey(stageId)`'s default key (`…/<stage>/output.json`): read another stage's output from `workflowContext`. What the body did itself (step rows, `ctx.storage`, logs, external calls) is not undone.

A second `step.signal` whose ledger row carries the same timestamp as the first one's no longer replays the first wake and leaves the stage `SUSPENDED`. The wake claim told two signal rows apart by their completion time in milliseconds alone, so a row written after a ledger reset in the same millisecond, or on an adapter with a coarser clock, looked like the delivery that had already woken the stage. The claim now also records the row's creation time, position and attempt, and the ledger reset of an explicit `job.execute` re-run releases the claims of the signal rows it drops, once the clear has committed, so a signal sent again while the clear runs or after it fails wakes nothing twice. The release happens only on that path: a reset by a host still on an earlier release during a rolling deploy, and a `run.redrive` on a ledger without `clearExcept` (which drops completed signal rows without releasing their claims), can still lose the wake of a second signal whose row has the same timestamp, creation time, position and attempt. A claim stored by an earlier release carries only the delivery time and stays valid. No port or schema changes.

Custom hosts: pass the job's `attempt` and `maxAttempts` to `job.execute`, or call `executeJobWithHeartbeat`. A dispatch without `attempt` is treated as an explicit rerun of a `FAILED` stage, so a redelivery through such a host still resets the stage and runs its completed steps again. The documented examples now pass them.
