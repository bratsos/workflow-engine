A job delivered again after its stage already ended `FAILED` no longer reruns the stage. A stage is `FAILED` when its final attempt threw or its error was deterministic; if the host then lost the acknowledgement (a crash before `jobTransport.fail`, a lost lease, a delivery past the budget such as attempt 3 of 2, or a delivery within the budget after a deterministic failure on attempt 1 of 3), the redelivery reset the stage's step ledger and ran the completed steps again: a receipt with an external key ran a second time under the same key, or, with `onReclaim: "fail"`, its successful row was turned into a failure that replaced the real error. `job.execute` now answers a delivery that carries `attempt` and finds its stage `FAILED` with `outcome: "failed"`, the stored error, `willRetry: false` and no events, before it starts anything: the host fails the job and the run fails once with the stage's error, and no retry is scheduled; the stage, its ledger and its events are left as the first delivery wrote them. A retry in budget after a retryable failure is unchanged (the stage is `PENDING`, completed steps replay), and a direct `job.execute` dispatch without `attempt` still starts a `FAILED` stage again as an explicit re-run.

An attempt that was overtaken while its body ran no longer writes over the stage, and its output no longer replaces the output of the attempt that took over. `job.execute` fences the stage write that ends an attempt (completed, suspended, a pending retry or `FAILED`) on the stage version its start wrote, folded into that write's `WHERE`. The stage stays the attempt's while it is `RUNNING` under the same record, `attempt` and `startedAt`: another delivery after a lost lease stamps a `startedAt` strictly later than the one it replaces (whatever the clocks say), and a redrive, `run.rerunFrom`, a cancel or `run.reapStuck` changes the status or the attempt. A write that lost only to something else, such as `step.signal` nudging `nextPollAt` (on any release), is repeated on the stored version. So after a cancel and a redrive a late failure can neither mark the reopened stage `FAILED` (which the redrive's own job would then have taken for a dead job) nor complete it, and after a lost lease the stale attempt cannot commit over the delivery that took over. The late result is discarded with `ghost: true` and `ghostReason: "orphan"`, and its host's fenced acknowledgement leaves the new owner's job alone.

Stage output and artifacts are now first saved under keys of the attempt's own, `workflow-v2/<type>/<run>/<stage>/attempts/<id>/output.json` and `…/attempts/<id>/artifacts/<name>.json`, in `job.execute` and in both completion paths of `stage.pollSuspended`. Only the committed stage write points `outputData` at them, so an attempt or poll that loses its fence (also on earlier releases, where it overwrote the winner's blob under the shared per-stage key and the next stage read it) never changes what the next stage, `loadWorkflowContext` or any reader of the pointer gets. The loser's keys are deleted afterwards, best-effort, unless some `outputData` points at them. After its write commits, the winner also copies its output to the stage's fixed key `…/<stage>/output.json` (the default `ctx.storage.getStageKey(stageId)`) and its artifacts to `…/<stage>/artifacts/<name>.json`, as earlier releases wrote them, and a loser never writes them. The copy is best-effort and skipped unless the stage's pointer still names this attempt when re-read just before it, so the fixed keys can briefly lag behind the committed output (an older winner's late copy) or miss it (a failed copy, logged as a WARN on the run; a delivery that finds the stage COMPLETED copies it again); the pointer (`workflowContext`) is the authoritative source. Pointers stored by an earlier release name the old per-stage key and are read as they are. A redrive that reopens a stage keeps its earlier attempts' own blobs, so a superseded attempt's archived pointer still names its output until `run.purge`. `saveStageOutput` and `saveStageArtifacts` take an optional attempt key (a random one by default). What the body did itself (step rows, `ctx.storage`, logs, external calls) is not undone.

`InMemoryWorkflowPersistence.withTransaction` now rolls back every write of a transaction that throws, as the Prisma adapter does, and marks its transaction view `outboxDeliveryDeferred`. The rollback undoes only the transaction's own writes: a write made outside it meanwhile is kept, also to a row the transaction wrote before it. A failed write in a transaction (for example a signal wake whose outbox write failed) no longer leaves an idempotency key in progress for every later call.

A second `step.signal` whose ledger row carries the same timestamp as the first one's no longer replays the first wake and leaves the stage `SUSPENDED`. The wake claim told two signal rows apart by their completion time in milliseconds alone, so a row written after a ledger reset in the same millisecond, or on an adapter with a coarser clock, looked like the delivery that had already woken the stage. The claim now also records the row's creation time, position and attempt, and the ledger reset of an explicit `job.execute` re-run releases the claims of the signal rows it drops, once the clear has committed, so a signal sent again while the clear runs or after it fails wakes nothing twice. The release happens only on that path: a reset by a host still on an earlier release during a rolling deploy, and a `run.redrive` on a ledger without `clearExcept` (which drops completed signal rows without releasing their claims), can still lose the wake of a second signal whose row has the same timestamp, creation time, position and attempt. A claim stored by an earlier release carries only the delivery time and stays valid. No port or schema changes.

Custom hosts: pass the job's `attempt` and `maxAttempts` to `job.execute`, or call `executeJobWithHeartbeat`. A dispatch without `attempt` is treated as an explicit rerun of a `FAILED` stage, so a redelivery through such a host still resets the stage and runs its completed steps again. The documented examples now pass them.
